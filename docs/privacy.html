Sendline privacy policy

Effective 2026-10-19, updated 2026-10-19 for recipe import and barcode lookup

Sendline is an offline app. It has no account and no server. Nothing you enter leaves your phone unless you export it yourself. The exceptions are two features you switch on: recipe import and barcode lookup, described below.

What the app stores

  • The program you set up: dates, weights, calorie and protein targets, meals, exercises, checkpoints, reminder times.
  • What you log each day: which day type you picked, items you checked off, weigh-ins, lift sets, meal picks.
  • Progress photos, if you take them. They are saved in the app's private storage and are never uploaded.
  • Derived numbers such as XP, badges and your chain, computed from the logs above.

All of this lives in app-private storage on the device. Other apps cannot read it.

If you switch on weekly backups in Settings, Data, the app writes a copy of the above (without photos) into the one folder you picked with the system picker, once a week. It goes nowhere else; what happens to that folder afterwards (for example a sync app you use) is up to you.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking of any kind.
  • No network access unless you switch on Online features in Settings, and then only what is listed under "Recipe import" and "Barcode lookup".
  • No account, no sign-in, no email collection.

Recipe import

Off by default. When you paste a recipe link into New meal and allow it, the app downloads that one page from that website (following a short link to the page it points at, HTTPS only), and reads the recipe out of it (name, servings, protein and calories when the page lists them). The request goes to that site the way a browser visit would, so that site sees your IP address. Nothing about you or your data is sent, and the app makes no other connections. Switch it off any time under Settings, Data, Online features. The code that does it is public.

Barcode lookup

Off by default. When you scan a product barcode in New meal and allow it, the app sends the digits of that barcode, and nothing else, to Open Food Facts (world.openfoodfacts.org), a public food database, and reads the product's name, serving size, protein and calories from the answer. Open Food Facts sees your IP address the way any website visit would. The camera reads the barcode on the phone; no picture leaves it. Switch it off any time under Settings, Data, Online features. The code that does it is public.

Ingredients

The ingredient search in New meal and Edit meal uses a food table bundled inside the app (USDA FoodData Central, public domain). It works offline and sends nothing.

Permissions

  • Internet: only for recipe import and barcode lookup, only while you have them switched on, only for the page you paste or the barcode you scan.
  • Camera: only when you choose to take a progress photo, scan a barcode or read a nutrition label. Photos stay on the device, and the barcode and the label are read on the phone; no picture leaves it.
  • Notifications: one daily weigh-in reminder and one "pick a day" reminder, both optional.
  • Exact alarms: so reminders fire at the time you chose. If you do not allow it, reminders still fire, just less precisely.
  • Vibrate: haptic ticks when you check items, switchable off in Settings.

Backups and export

Settings has Export, which writes a JSON or zip file and hands it to an app you pick (your file manager, Drive, email). Where that file goes is up to you and that app. Android's own device backup may also include the app's data under your Google account settings; you control that in Android Settings, not in Sendline.

Deleting your data

Settings has Reset program, which wipes every log, photo and setting. Uninstalling the app removes everything as well.

Health note

Sendline is a logbook and a scoreboard, not medical advice. The targets you set are yours. Talk to a doctor before a diet if you have a condition, and stop if you feel unwell.
